Academic integrity is the commitment to honesty, trust, fairness, respect, and responsibility in scholarly work. All submitted coursework, assignments, term papers, and laboratory reports must reflect the student's original effort and authentic understanding of the subject matter.

Plagiarism constitutes a severe academic violation defined as presenting another author's ideas, text, data, or intellectual property as one's own without clear and accurate attribution. Plagiarism includes verbatim copying of text, improper paraphrasing without citation, submitting work generated by automated software tools without disclosure, and self-plagiarism where previous coursework is submitted without permission.

Proper citation guidelines require students to acknowledge all external sources of information using standard referencing formats such as APA, IEEE, MLA, or Harvard styles. In-text citations must directly accompany direct quotations, summary statements, or adapted statistical data, alongside a complete bibliography at the end of the manuscript.

Submissions are evaluated through automated similarity detection software to assess textual overlap against academic repositories, published research papers, and reference documents. High similarity indices without appropriate citation will result in disciplinary action including grade penalties, course failure, and academic review.
